---
title: "Dunking biscuits"
description: "Biscuits made with baker's ammonia, from a dough that rests overnight in the fridge."
date: 2026-11-16
cook: "15 min"
oven: "180 °C (355 °F), no fan"
tags: ["desserts", "biscuits"]
ingredients:
  - { q: "430 g (15 oz)", i: "Flour" }
  - { q: "3", i: "Eggs" }
  - { q: "50 g (1¾ oz)", i: "Neutral vegetable oil" }
  - { q: "170 g (6 oz)", i: "Sugar" }
  - { q: "7 g (¼ oz)", i: "Baker's ammonia (ammonium carbonate)" }
  - { q: "½ tsp", i: "Vanilla extract (optional)" }
  - { q: "as needed", i: "Granulated sugar, for rolling" }
---

1. Beat the eggs with the sugar for a few minutes, then add the oil and the sifted flour mixed with the baker's ammonia. Work the dough well until everything is combined.
2. Cover the bowl with cling film or a lid and let the dough rest **in the fridge** overnight (or longer). Let it come back to room temperature before working it.
3. Shape the dough into finger-wide ropes, cut them into 7-8 cm (about 3 in) pieces, roll them in granulated sugar and place them well apart on a baking tray lined with parchment paper: they spread as they bake.
4. Bake at 180 °C (355 °F), in a conventional oven with no fan, for about 15 minutes. Wedge a spoon in the oven door to let the steam out. You will smell strong ammonia while they bake: that is normal, and nothing of it remains in the biscuits.

**Notes**

- A faster way: roll the dough out into a rectangle about 1-1.5 cm (½ in) thick, sprinkle with granulated sugar, turn it over and dust the other side with more sugar. Cut strips 1-1.5 cm (½ in) wide with a pizza wheel and place them on the tray.
- The dough is fairly stiff: with only 3 eggs and 50 g (1¾ oz) of oil for 430 g (15 oz) of flour, that is normal. If it is too crumbly to shape, add a spoonful of milk.
- The ammonia smell while baking is normal: baker's ammonia works well for thin, dry biscuits like these.
- Store them in an airtight container: the smell disappears within a few hours.

**Variations**

- Lemon or orange zest in the dough.
- Aniseed.
- A spoonful of cocoa mixed into the flour, for chocolate biscuits.

> Tasty!
