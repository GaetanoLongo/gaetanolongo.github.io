**Recipe for two loaves, made with licoli (liquid sourdough) fed with type 0 flour.** It can be baked with or without a Dutch oven (cast-iron pot). Quantities rounded to tens of grams.

Totals, licoli included: flour 1250 g (2.8 lb): 940 g semolina + 310 g (11 oz) type 0 flour; water 840 g (30 oz); hydration 67%; semolina to flour ratio 3 : 1. The licoli is at 100% hydration (half flour, half water).

1. **Autolyse, 30-40 min.** Mix all the flours with 650 g (23 oz) of the water (not all of it) and cover.
2. **Dough.** Add the licoli, the remaining water little by little, and the salt last. Knead until the dough is smooth and elastic, not too tight. Dough temperature: 22-24 °C (72-75 °F).
3. **Short but active bulk fermentation (about 2 h at 22 °C / 72 °F).** Do 3 folds to even out the holes (step-by-step instructions below):
   - after 30 min: gentle letter fold
   - after 60 min: coil fold
   - after 90 min: coil fold + light degassing

   **This is the key step.** During the last fold, gently press down the big bubbles, but don't batter the dough: you are redistributing the gas, not deflating it. End of bulk: volume +30-40%, not doubled.
4. **Dividing and preshaping (compulsory).** Divide into 2 pieces of about 1 kg (2.2 lb). Preshape like this: flatten slightly, fold into a parcel, seal underneath, tighten just enough. Rest: 20-25 min, covered. Here you break the giant pockets without losing the fine gas.
5. **Final shaping, anti huge holes.** Shape the loaf: flatten slightly, press the centre, close the flaps well, with progressive, not violent tension. Goal: an even skin, air only spread out, no pockets.
6. **Second rise in the fridge.** Floured banneton, well covered, at 4-8 °C (39-46 °F) for 8-12 hours. In the fridge the yeasts slow down, the gases even out and no giant bubbles form.
7. **Baking, with or without a Dutch oven (cast-iron pot).** Conventional oven (no fan) at 230 °C (445 °F). Bake the loaf cold, straight from the fridge. Slash: a real 1 cm (⅜ in) deep, with the blade at an angle.
   - **With a Dutch oven:** the pot, with its lid on, must be preheated: put it in the oven while it heats up to 230 °C (445 °F). Put the loaf into the hot pot and bake 15 min at 230 °C (445 °F) with the lid on. Then take the lid off, so the moisture can escape, and continue with the lid off at 200-205 °C (390-400 °F) for 25-30 min.
   - **Without a Dutch oven, on a baking tray:** bake 15 min at 230 °C (445 °F), then open the oven door for about 10 seconds, only to let the moisture escape. Lower the temperature to 200-205 °C (390-400 °F), close the door and continue for 25-30 min, keeping the door ajar for the last 10 min.

   Core temperature: 96-98 °C (205-208 °F).

## The techniques, step by step

You only need two folds: the gentle letter fold and the coil fold.

### Gentle letter fold

*Initial strengthening, gas control.*

**When**

- First fold of the bulk fermentation.
- Dough still young, with little structure.

**Preparation**

- Work surface lightly dampened (not floured).
- Clean hands, dry or just slightly damp.

**Steps**

- Turn the dough out onto the surface.
- Flatten it slightly with your hands: don't press it down, just spread it.
- Take a short side and bring it towards the centre.
- Take the opposite side and bring it over the first (like folding a letter).
- Turn the dough 90°.
- Repeat the two folding steps once only (short side to the centre, opposite side over it).
- Turn the dough seam side down.
- Put it back in the bowl.

**How it should look**

- More compact.
- Surface already smoother.
- No tears.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- Pulling hard.
- Doing it more than once.
- Pressing out all the gas.

### Coil fold

*Gentle strengthening and gas redistribution.*

**When**

- Second and third fold.
- Dough already elastic.
- Medium-high hydration.

**Preparation**

- Dough in the bowl.
- Slightly wet hands.
- No work surface.

**Steps**

- Slide both hands under the centre of the dough, palms up.
- Lift slowly: the sides fall down by gravity.
- Let the dough fold under itself.
- Set it back in the bowl: it is now taller and gathered.
- Turn the bowl 90°.
- Repeat the previous steps.
- In total 2-3 lifts, no more.

**How it should look**

- Dough that "stands up" more.
- Surface taut but not pulled.
- Volume preserved.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- Yanking.
- Lifting suddenly.
- Doing it on cold or stiff dough.

### Targeted degassing (only if needed)

Only on the last coil: with your fingers, press down 2-3 big bubbles, not the whole mass. This prevents huge holes under the crust.

### Preshaping

*Right after dividing.*

**When**

- End of the bulk fermentation.
- Dough relaxed but alive.
- Volume about +30-40%.

**Preparation**

- Work surface lightly floured (very little).
- Cut the dough into 2 equal pieces, without tearing.

**Steps**

- Take one piece.
- Turn it over with the smooth side down.
- Flatten it slightly, with open hands.
- Bring one side towards the centre.
- Bring the opposite side over the first.
- Repeat with the other two sides: you get a "parcel".
- Turn the piece seam side down.
- With your hands cupped, round it gently, with short circular movements.
- Don't squeeze hard.

**Correct result**

- Round shape.
- Surface already tidy.
- Light tension.

**Rest**

- Cover and let rest 20-25 minutes.
- This rest is crucial: it relaxes the gluten and avoids tears during shaping.

### Final shaping: the loaf

**Preparation**

- Work surface lightly floured.
- Take the preshaped dough after its rest.

**Steps**

- Turn the preshaped dough over, seam up.
- Flatten gently, especially in the centre, without pressing the edges.
- Bring the top side towards the centre.
- Bring the bottom side over it, sealing with your fingers.
- Bring the two lateral sides towards the centre and close well.
- Pull the dough towards you while closing underneath (tensioning movement).
- Turn 90° and repeat 2-3 times, with progressive tension.
- Close the base well, pinching it lightly.

**Correct result**

- Smooth, taut surface.
- No tears.
- Loaf that "stands up".

**What not to do**

- Tearing.
- Squeezing suddenly.
- Leaving giant bubbles under the skin.

**After shaping**

- Put it straight into the banneton, seam up, cover well and put it in the fridge.

### Confirmation signs

- The loaf doesn't spread out.
- It springs back slowly if you press it with a finger.
- The surface is elastic, not stiff.
