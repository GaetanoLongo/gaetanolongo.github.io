Starting quantities for 500 ml of alcohol. To change the amounts or the strength use the **calculator** below: with these proportions the limoncello comes out at around 32% vol (the calculator's estimate).

1. **Zest.** Wash untreated lemons with an edible peel and peel only the yellow part (the flavedo), without the bitter white pith (the albedo). You need about 30 g of zest for every 100 ml of alcohol. Cutting it into thin strips speeds up the extraction.
2. **Infusion.** Put the zest in an airtight jar with all of the alcohol (not just part of it), at room temperature and in the dark. Shake once or twice a day. One day extracts the main aromas, three days the secondary ones too.
3. **Quick version.** Close the jar with the alcohol and zest and put it in a pot of water at 50 °C (120 °F), with the lid on. After 2 hours the water is barely lukewarm and the extract is ready. Don't heat it more, so as not to alter the flavour.
4. **Strain.** Remove the zest and filter the extract.
5. **Syrup.** Dissolve the sugar in the water. As a starting point use water equal to the volume of the alcohol (120% or more for a less alcoholic liqueur) and sugar at 80% of the weight of the water, that is 80 g per 100 ml. Combine the syrup with the extract.
6. **Rest.** Let it rest for one or two weeks: the flavour matures.
7. **Serving.** Serve it very cold. You can even keep it in the freezer: the alcohol and sugar stop it from freezing.

**Notes**

- Don't add lemon juice: the acid environment degrades the essential oils.
- If you dissolve the sugar hot, let the syrup cool before adding it to the extract (my addition, not in the source).
- Three versions compared, with the same ingredients: one day of infusion gives the freshest aroma, closest to the whole lemon; three days give a fuller aroma, the most similar to a commercial limoncello; two hours at 50 °C (120 °F) give the most intense aroma and flavour.
- There is no perfect recipe: commercial limoncello has between 25% and 37% vol alcohol (about 30% on average) and between 18% and 29% sugar. Raise or lower the alcohol and sugar to your taste.
- This is a summary: for the science (the aromas, the maceration times, the strength calculation) read Dario Bressanini's original article, which is in Italian.
